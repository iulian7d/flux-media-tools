Common Pet Medications — Dosing Guide

Veterinary medications are typically dosed by body weight (mg/kg). The doses shown are based on published veterinary references including Plumb's Veterinary Drug Handbook and BSAVA Small Animal Formulary. Individual animals may require adjustments — always follow your vet's specific instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my dog's medication dosage?

Most vet medications are dosed in mg per kg of body weight. Multiply your pet's weight in kg by the mg/kg dose rate to get the total mg needed per dose. Then divide by the tablet or liquid concentration to find how many tablets or ml to give. For example: amoxicillin at 10 mg/kg for a 10 kg dog = 100 mg per dose. If tablets are 250 mg, give 0.4 tablets per dose.

Can I give my dog human medications?

Most human medications are not safe for pets. Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs can cause severe kidney and gastrointestinal damage in dogs. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is fatal to cats and harmful to dogs. Aspirin is generally not recommended. Even medications that appear similar to vet drugs may be differently formulated. Never give human medication to your pet without explicit veterinary instruction.

Is paracetamol safe for cats?

No — paracetamol (Tylenol/acetaminophen) is extremely toxic to cats and can be fatal even in very small amounts. Cats lack the liver enzyme needed to break it down safely. It is also harmful to dogs. If you suspect your pet has ingested paracetamol, contact your vet or animal poison control (UK: 01202 509000 — Animal Poison Line; US: ASPCA 888-426-4435) immediately.

What is carprofen used for in dogs?

Carprofen (Rimadyl, Canidryl) is an NSAID used to treat pain and inflammation in dogs — particularly osteoarthritis, post-surgical pain, and soft tissue injuries. It is not safe for cats. The standard dose is 2–4 mg/kg once daily. Dogs on long-term carprofen require regular blood tests to monitor liver and kidney function.

How much metronidazole do I give my dog?

Metronidazole (Flagyl) is used for gastrointestinal upsets, giardia, and certain anaerobic infections. The typical dose in dogs is 10–25 mg/kg twice daily for 5–7 days depending on the condition. It has a very bitter taste — giving it wrapped in food or a pill pocket helps. Cats can also take metronidazole but at vet-directed doses. Never give more than prescribed as high doses can cause neurological side effects.
