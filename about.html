Who builds this

My name is Iulian Duma-Trunchi and I build Flux Media Systems. By day I work as a software engineer, and I've always liked automating things — taking a fiddly, repetitive task and turning it into a tool that gives you a clear answer in seconds. That's really all this site is: the calculators I wished existed. Accurate, fast, honest about their limits, and free.

I want to be straight about what I am and what I'm not. I'm not a financial adviser, an accountant, or a solicitor, and I don't pretend to be. What I'm genuinely good at is engineering: reading the official rules carefully (HMRC, gov.uk, and the rest), turning them into a tool that does the maths correctly, testing it hard against the government's own figures, and showing my working so you can check it. Where the government publishes its own calculator, mine is built to match it.

Because of that, everything here is general information, not professional advice. For a decision with real money or legal consequences on the line, confirm the result with a qualified professional. And if you ever spot a figure that looks wrong or out of date, the contact page comes straight to me — I fix errors quickly and I'm grateful for the heads-up.

What this is

Flux Media Systems is built around one idea: the number you need to make a good decision should be free, fast, and honest. Too often the useful answer is buried behind a sign-up wall, padded into a 3,000-word article written to rank rather than inform, or scattered across a forum thread from 2017. This site exists to cut through that — to give you the actual figure and a plain explanation in under two minutes.

What you'll find here

Free calculators and plain-English guides, focused on UK money and property decisions:

  • Property & Home — stamp duty, mortgage affordability, loan-to-value, home equity, rent vs buy, rental yield, moving and renovation costs.
  • Money & Finance — mortgage and loan repayments, investment growth, retirement, take-home salary, inheritance tax, freelance day rates, late-payment interest.
  • Work & Pay — statutory redundancy pay and take-home pay after tax and National Insurance.
  • Everyday tools — a few handy non-finance utilities: an age calculator, a discount calculator, a QR-code generator, and a password generator.

All tools are free to use, with no sign-up required.

Where the numbers come from

Calculators use official published figures wherever possible — HMRC for tax bands, Stamp Duty and Inheritance Tax; gov.uk for statutory redundancy pay and employment rules; the DWP for the State Pension; and the Bank of England base rate for statutory late-payment interest. Each tool states the source it uses and the tax year it applies to. There's a full write-up of how every calculator is built, tested, and kept current on the methodology page.

Most tools are built for the UK first, with US figures included where they clearly apply (for example, take-home pay and mortgage repayments). Where rules differ by jurisdiction, both are covered and labelled.

How it's funded

The site carries display advertising through Google AdSense — that's what covers the running costs. There are no affiliate links, no sponsored recommendations, and no products being pushed. Nothing a calculator tells you is influenced by any third party.
